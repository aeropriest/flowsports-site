Sound familiar?

Great in the lesson. Gone in the match.

You hit it clean all hour with your coach. Then Saturday comes, the score matters, and the same forehand sails long. It isn't that you forgot how. Lessons and matches are two different sports.

With your coach

  • Same ball, same height, same pace, fed to your strike zone
  • You're set and balanced before every swing
  • Fifty forehands in a row, so the groove builds
  • Someone watches every swing and tells you straight away

In a real match

  • Every ball is different: high, low, fast, short, spinning
  • You're on the run, late, off balance
  • One shot, then something else entirely
  • Pressure on, nobody watching, old habits back
You can't fix what you can't see.

Under pressure your body goes back to what it trusts. The late contact, the short follow-through, the arm doing all the work. You don't feel it, because what you feel you're doing and what you're really doing are not the same.

Your coach sees your lesson. Nobody sees your match. FlowTennis does. Film the tennis that counts, then watch every shot slowed down, put it next to a pro, and take what you find back to your next lesson.

How it works

Prop. Play. Watch.

01

Prop your phone

On the fence or your bag, sideways, facing your side of the court. Press record and forget about it. The screen dims to save battery.

02

Play your match

An hour, two hours, whatever you play. FlowTennis listens for every strike and watches your body as you hit.

03

Watch just your shots

The walking, picking up balls and waiting are cut out. You're left with every swing, labelled, scored and in slow motion.

Side by side with a pro

Your swing. Their swing. Same frame.

Pick any of your shots and play it next to a pro's, lined up at the same moment. Drag through preparation, contact and follow-through and the difference shows itself. Is your contact in front? Did your shoulders turn? Now you can see it.

AI coach

Ask about any shot.

Tap the chat button on any swing and ask what you'd ask your coach. It looks at that exact shot and gives you a straight answer and one thing to work on.

What should I fix first on this one?
Your contact is late. The ball is level with your hip, not out in front, so your arm is doing the work. Try this: meet it a racquet-length ahead of your front foot and let your shoulders turn through.

Example answer. The coach only talks about what the camera can measure, your body and the strike. It won't guess at speed, spin or depth.
